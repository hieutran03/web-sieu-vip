# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Three overlapping audiences at the same deal table:

- **Japanese capital allocators** — funds, developers, and trading houses scouting Vietnam entry points. They scan for credible thesis framing, geographic clarity, and counterparty names they recognize.
- **Vietnamese developers and advisors** — local real estate developers (Vinhomes, Becamex, Nam Long etc.) or investment advisors sourcing Japanese partners. They read the tool to understand how their market reads to foreign capital.
- **Internal research and advisory team** — analysts who built and maintain this; they use it to run analysis, brief clients, and present in rooms.

Primary scenario: analyst opens the tool at their desk for research, then switches to presentation mode to walk a client or counterpart through findings in a meeting.

## Product Purpose

Vietnam Japan Capital Atlas is an investment intelligence platform that maps and interprets Japanese FDI flows into Vietnam across six sectors (Urban, Industrial, Logistics, Retail, Energy, Public capital) for the period 2024–2026. Success looks like: a room of stakeholders walking away convinced the capital thesis is credible and geographically grounded — not just interesting.

## Brand Personality

Refined, international, nuanced. The tool sits at the intersection of Japanese institutional discipline and Vietnamese market dynamism. It should feel like a commissioned strategy report with interactive precision — not a startup tool, not a government portal.

Three words: **measured confidence**.

## Anti-references

- **Government data portals** — Austere, under-designed, flat tables, public-sector statistics aesthetic. This tool must feel like it was worth commissioning.
- **Startup investor decks** — YC-style pitch energy: oversized metric hero, "Series A" confidence, animated bar charts, hype framing. The thesis here is substantiated, not sold.

## Design Principles

1. **Evidence carries the argument.** Numbers, coordinates, and company names do the persuasion. Design frames them; it doesn't substitute for them. No decorative data viz.
2. **Legible at presentation distance.** Charts and map elements are shown in rooms on projectors. Critical labels, legends, and data must read from 2 meters without leaning in.
3. **Cross-cultural neutrality.** Three reading cultures (Vietnamese, English, Japanese) share this surface. Typography, hierarchy, and density must feel native to all three — no cultural aesthetic that feels foreign or condescending to any audience.
4. **Presence without loudness.** This is a bespoke product with visual confidence. Refinement is not restraint. The palette, weight, and proportion can be distinctive — but nothing that distracts from the data during a meeting.
5. **Geography first.** The map is the spine of the argument. Design should orient users spatially before analytically. Regional framing (North / Central / South) is a primary cognitive organizing principle, not a secondary filter.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Multi-language: Vietnamese (primary), English, Japanese — all content must render correctly in all three.
- Color used for data encoding (sector categories on the map) must remain distinguishable for common color blindness types (deuteranopia / protanopia). Use shape + color, not color alone.
- No accessibility level formally specified; aim for WCAG AA on text contrast as a floor.
